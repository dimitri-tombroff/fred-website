## The Usual Story (and Why It Misses the Point)

When people talk about AI in engineering, the conversation almost always lands on code generation, test writing, or PR reviews. Those are real gains. But they are the smallest part of the story.

What we discovered — separately, on two very different projects — is that an AI assistant becomes genuinely powerful when you bring it into the *whole* engineering journey: the messy upstream work that happens before a single line of code is written.

Roadmaps. Backlogs. RFC drafts. Sprint planning. Cross-team alignment. Risk identification. That is where the leverage is.

---

## Two Projects, One Observation

**Fred / Swift** (Dimitri & Simon) is a large, multi-team agentic platform. It has a formal architecture contract, a layered backlog, versioned RFCs, a PMO board, and a task-ID registry. The kind of complexity that normally requires significant coordination overhead.

**[Yahia's project — name TBD]** is a leaner, more focused application. Different scope, different team size. But Yahia arrived at the same practice: using Claude not just to write code, but to hold context, draft specs, flag contradictions, and drive alignment.

The interesting question is: *why did two independent teams, working on very different things, end up in the same place?*
